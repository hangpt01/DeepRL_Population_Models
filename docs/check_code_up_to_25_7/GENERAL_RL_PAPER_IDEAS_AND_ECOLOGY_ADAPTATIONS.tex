\section{Why this document exists}

This document explains four general offline reinforcement-learning methods used in the
hidden-demographics real-ecology benchmark. It is written for a reader who does not inspect the
code and may not remember the papers in detail. Its goals are to:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item remind the reader of each paper's basic algorithmic idea;
  \item explain the mechanism retained in the current implementation;
  \item distinguish necessary ecological adaptations from computational approximations;
  \item state honestly what is \emph{not} reproduced;
  \item provide tables that can be converted directly into supervisor slides; and
  \item identify the most important future improvements.
\end{enumerate}

The four registered methods are:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item RefPlan-inspired posterior-adaptive planning;
  \item OGSRL-inspired guarded constrained policy optimization;
  \item BA-MCTS-inspired Bayes-adaptive tree search; and
  \item ensemble value-disagreement pessimism, motivated by Delphic pessimism only at the idea
  level.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\fcolorbox{warnred}{softgray}{\parbox{0.94\linewidth}{
\textbf{Important naming rule.} The first three methods retain recognizable paper-defining
mechanisms but remain benchmark adaptations. The fourth is \emph{not} a Delphic reproduction: it
does not fit latent causal worlds and its variance must not be called Delphic uncertainty.
}}

\section{Ecological decision problem}

\subsection{What is hidden?}

The central benchmark question is whether a general learner can make useful management decisions
without being told the demographic structure. In hidden mode, methods do not receive intrinsic or
action-specific demographic parameters, carrying capacities, the true family label, the private
safety threshold, or native mechanistic solver access. During deployment they receive noisy public
survey observations, action history, and public management information.

This changes the learning problem from ``optimize a known population model'' to ``infer enough
predictive structure from an offline log to act under structural uncertainty.''

\section{RefPlan-inspired: reflect on evidence, then plan}

\subsection{Paper reminder}

Reflect-then-Plan views offline model-based planning through a ``doubly Bayesian'' lens
\cite{refplan2025}. First, a conservative offline policy provides a prior over plausible plans.
Second, the agent maintains a belief over dynamics models and updates it from real deployment
observations. The belief is marginalized into MPPI-style planning, and uncertain predicted returns
are penalized.

The key identity condition is:
\[
\text{deployment evidence}\longrightarrow\text{model posterior}
\longrightarrow\text{different plan}.
\]
Computing a posterior but ignoring it during planning would not be RefPlan in substance.

\subsection{Current algorithm}

The current method fits two distinct public objects:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item a five-member bootstrap dynamics ensemble; and
  \item a standardized multinomial behavior-policy prior.
\end{enumerate}

The policy prior is mixed with a uniform floor:
\[
\widetilde\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(a\mid h)
=0.9\,\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(a\mid h)+0.1\,\mathrm{Uniform}(a).
\]
For every candidate plan, the method samples an action from this prior, propagates a
posterior-predictive public state, updates the simulated public history, reevaluates the prior, and
continues through the planning horizon. Thus later actions depend on simulated history rather than
reusing only the root-state action distribution.

For candidate sequence $u$, model-member returns $R_m(u)$, and current posterior $w_m$, the
reflected score is approximately
\[
S(u)=\sum_m w_mR_m(u)
-\lambda_{\mathrm{ref}}
\sqrt{\sum_m w_m\left(R_m(u)-\sum_jw_jR_j(u)\right)^2}.
\]
The highest-scoring sequence supplies the next action. After the real public transition, member
weights are updated by likelihood and planning starts again.

\section{OGSRL-inspired: optimize inside a guarded model}

\subsection{Paper reminder}

Offline Guarded Safe RL learns a guardian for the in-distribution state-action region, constructs a
guarded transition model, and then performs constrained policy optimization in that model
\cite{ogsrl2025}. The original experiments use KDE/kNN components and CPO. The paper permits other
constrained optimizers, but a trained multi-step constrained policy is central to the method.

Conceptually:
\[
\max_\pi V_r^\pi
\quad\text{subject to}\quad
V_{\mathrm{OOD}}^\pi\leq d_{\mathrm{OOD}},
\qquad
V_c^\pi\leq d_c.
\]

\subsection{Current algorithm}

The current method fits the shared public dynamics ensemble and a public kNN support guardian. It
then trains a linear softmax actor, critic, and two Lagrange multipliers for 30 iterations. One
constraint measures OOD exposure; the other uses a public low-abundance proxy because the private
ecological safety threshold is hidden.

Let $s_{\mathrm{low}}$ be the 20th percentile of positive training observations. The public step
cost is
\[
c(o')=\mathrm{clip}\left(\frac{s_{\mathrm{low}}-o'}{s_{\mathrm{low}}},0,1\right).
\]
The normalized 25-step occupancy is
\[
C_{25}=\sum_{t=0}^{24}
\frac{\gamma^t}{\sum_{j=0}^{24}\gamma^j}\,c(o_{t+1}),
\qquad \gamma=0.95.
\]
The budget is the arithmetic mean of $C_{25}$ over complete training behavior episodes. There is no
post-hoc floor or cap.

During training and deployment, cost is applied inside each sampled predictive path before
averaging. Deployment compares every forced first action with 256 deterministic-common-random-number
paths, balanced over five model members, and follows the actor for continuation actions. The
estimated quantity is
\[
\mathbb E_{\text{belief, member, predictive residual, actor}}[C_{25}].
\]

\section{BA-MCTS-inspired: update model belief inside search}

\subsection{Paper reminder}

Bayes-Adaptive MCTS models offline MBRL as a Bayes-adaptive MDP
\cite{bamcts2026}. The planning state includes both physical state and belief over possible world
models. A simulated observation changes that belief, so search can value actions partly because of
what they may reveal.

The ideal Bayesian update is
\[
b_{t+1}(m)\propto b_t(m)
p_m(o_{t+1}\mid h_t,a_t).
\]
The paper combines this idea with PUCT, double progressive widening for continuous problems, deep
ensembles, and an outer policy/value distillation loop. It also explains why fixing one root-sampled
model throughout a DPW rollout is insufficient for its method.

\subsection{Current algorithm}

The current planner uses five public dynamics members. Every simulated tree history carries its
own categorical member belief in log space. A model is sampled from the current node belief, a
public successor is generated, and member weights are updated from the simulated public transition
likelihood before recursion.

Management actions are already discrete, so action progressive widening is unnecessary. Continuous
survey observations are grouped into public tree buckets. At each real decision, the method runs
256 simulations to depth 8 and then replans after the next real observation.

better continuous-observation tree handling would make the system closer to the paper.

\section{Value-disagreement pessimism: Delphic-motivated only}

\subsection{Delphic paper reminder}

Delphic Offline RL addresses nonidentifiable hidden confounding
\cite{delphic2024}. It fits several latent causal worlds that are observationally compatible with
the same behavior data. A policy is evaluated in every retained world, Delphic uncertainty is the
cross-world variance in $Q$, and a pessimism penalty discourages decisions whose value depends on
unidentifiable counterfactual assumptions.

This ecology benchmark contains a relevant confounding pattern: the data-collection behavior policy
may use privileged true abundance while hidden methods see noisy surveys. Nevertheless, a true
Delphic adaptation would still require coherent latent behavior, transition, and reward worlds
fitted to the same observable trajectory distribution.

\subsection{Why the current method has a different name}

An earlier local construction generated disagreement by directly perturbing Q outside support.
That could manufacture the desired variance pattern and did not correspond to complete fitted
causal worlds. It was rejected. The current method therefore uses a neutral empirical estimator and
does not claim Delphic uncertainty.

For each of $M=20$ members, complete training episodes are resampled with replacement and an
independent conservative linear fitted-Q estimator is trained. Members differ only through their
bootstrap data and deterministic seed. No random term is added directly to Q.

With
\[
\overline Q(o,a)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M Q_m(o,a),
\qquad
V_Q(o,a)=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{m=1}^M
\left(Q_m(o,a)-\overline Q(o,a)\right)^2,
\]
the action score is
\[
S(o,a)=\overline Q(o,a)-0.1V_Q(o,a).
\]

The penalty is operational but weak: in audited Amur and vulture holdouts, it changed about
0.125--0.375\% of actions relative to pure ensemble-mean Q. This is a mechanism observation, not a
performance claim.

\paragraph{Primary settings.}
Twenty episode-bootstrap members, 35 fitted-Q iterations, CQL-style coefficient 0.5,
disagreement coefficient 0.1 in inverse-Q units, and discount 0.95.

\paragraph{Allowed claim.}
\adaptation{Episode-bootstrap conservative fitted-Q ensemble with empirical value-disagreement
pessimism; Delphic-motivated at the idea level only.}

\paragraph{Improvements.}
To claim a genuine Delphic adaptation, fit discrete-latent generative worlds by episode-level
marginal likelihood or ELBO, verify observable compatibility, and derive each $Q_w$ through that
world's fitted transition/reward model. Otherwise the honest next steps are nonlinear Q ensembles,
bootstrap-count sensitivity, and independently justified disagreement scaling.

\section{Verification and current experimental status}

The frozen Phase 2E runtime passed 203/203 tests. Verification includes:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item private-information intervention invariance through
  \method{fit -> act -> observe -> act};
  \item RefPlan posterior sensitivity and history-conditioned prior tests;
  \item OGSRL shared pathwise-cost, common-random-number, Jensen, dual, and action-risk tests;
  \item BA-MCTS in-tree posterior concentration and numerical-stability tests;
  \item bootstrap-membership, empirical-variance, no-direct-perturbation, and action-switch tests
  for EVD pessimism; and
  \item one-thread deterministic parity and strict return-blind receipt parsing.
\end{itemize}

A 64-row canary completed with 64/64 accepted validity receipts, no failures or retries, and no
outcome fields opened. The matched full package contains 576 rows and uses only
$\sigma_{\mathrm{obs}}\in\{0.1,0.2\}$. At the time of this document, it is prepared and validated;
performance results are not included here.

\section{Slide-ready summary}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Problem slide:} hidden $r$, $K$, family, and private safety threshold; only public
  offline observations and actions are available.
  \item \textbf{Method map:} RefPlan updates a dynamics posterior; OGSRL constrains an actor;
  BA-MCTS updates belief inside search; EVD penalizes unstable fitted value.
