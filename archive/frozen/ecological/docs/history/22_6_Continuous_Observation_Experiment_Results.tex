\begin{abstract}
This is the empirical companion to the continuous-state synthetic benchmark specification. It records the
\emph{realized} experiment: the standalone implementation in
\texttt{discrete\_action\_cont\_obser/}, every hyperparameter actually used (with
its meaning), the calibration/decision-gate outcomes, and the full
$384$-configuration result matrix over the four observation-noise levels. The
headline finding: at low-to-moderate survey noise ($\sobs\!\le\!0.2$) the general
dynamics-learning learners beat the stronger ecology baseline
$\max(\text{PLUS},\text{MOOR})$ in $33$--$83\%$ of cells, but at high noise
($\sobs{=}0.4$) that advantage \emph{collapses to $\approx 0$}: the single-model
mechanistic baseline (MOOR) and the guarded method (OGSRL) become best. We give
the per-method tables, the filter ablation (the shared learned filter helps the
general learners but not the mechanistic baselines), an analysis of why, and a
prioritized list of improvements.
\end{abstract}

\section{Realized configuration and how to read this companion}
The benchmark was implemented as a standalone, NumPy-only repository
(\texttt{discrete\_action\_cont\_obser/}) with no dependency on the discrete
Tier-1 code. The executed matrix is
\[
\underbrace{\{\text{allee},\text{theta},\text{regime}\}}_{3}\times
\underbrace{\{5\text{a},10\text{a}\}}_{2}\times
\underbrace{\sobs\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.4\}}_{4}\times
\underbrace{7\ \text{methods}}_{\text{MOPO,RefPlan,BA-MCTS,PLUS,MOOR,Delphic,OGSRL}}\times
\{\text{learned},\text{raw}\}
\]
$=336$ rows, plus $48$ Ricker-filter baseline-fidelity rows for PLUS/MOOR
(one per cell), $=\mathbf{384}$ runs. Evaluation uses five disjoint held-out seed
blocks $\{7001,7051,7101,7151,7201\}$, $50$ episodes each ($250$/cell), horizon
$50$, $\gamma{=}0.95$, with episode parameters/starts/noise streams paired across
methods. The dataset seed is $116$ and the data budget is $\sim$75k
trajectory-preserving transitions per cell. All $384$ runs completed; all $24$
cells satisfy the $[0.15,0.24]$ calibration band and the decision gate.

\paragraph{Compute (for reproducibility).} CPU only (the reference implementation
is pure NumPy; GPUs idle). Each row: \texttt{partition=comp}, \texttt{qos=rtq},
$1$ CPU, $8$\,GB, single-threaded BLAS, $8$\,h ceiling; array throttle $72$. The
longest single method is OGSRL ($\sim$2\,h/row); PLUS fit ($\sim$40\,min, a
Python-loop value iteration) dominates the mechanistic cost.

\section{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{(1) Observation noise is the decisive axis, and it reverses the ranking.}
At $\sobs{=}0$ the general learners lead (RefPlan $7.33$, MOPO $7.20$ vs.\ MOOR
$6.97$) and beat $\max(\text{PLUS},\text{MOOR})$ in up to $67\%$ of cells. As
$\sobs$ rises the learned advantage erodes in lockstep with filter RMSE, and at
$\sobs{=}0.4$ the beats-both rate is $\approx 0$ for every dynamics-learning
method. \emph{Under heavy survey noise, general offline MBRL does not beat the
mechanistic baselines in this benchmark} --- the central, and sobering, continuous-state synthetic benchmark
result.

\paragraph{(2) The single-model baseline MOOR is the surprise winner at high noise.}
MOOR is the best method overall under the learned filter ($7.23$) and
\emph{improves} with noise ($6.97\!\to\!7.80$) --- as do PLUS ($5.64\!\to\!6.56$)
and OGSRL ($4.37\!\to\!7.48$), so the rising trend is not unique to MOOR --- while
MOOR's collapse rate \emph{falls} ($.053\!\to\!.023$). Its
one conservative Ricker fit regulates abundance to a safe mid-band and is nearly
insensitive to which noisy observation arrives; the learned planners, by contrast,
propagate a degraded belief through a model and a multi-step MPC, compounding the
filter error. The per-episode-mixture argument that was supposed to hurt MOOR is
dominated, at high $\sobs$, by its robustness.

\paragraph{(3) OGSRL is the noise-robust safe learner.} OGSRL rises sharply with
noise ($4.37\!\to\!7.48$), is competitive with MOOR at $\sobs\!\ge\!0.2$, and has
the lowest unsafe occupancy at $\sobs{=}0.2$. Its hard-guardian fallback activates
far more as the belief degrades --- mean hard fallbacks per episode rise
$0.28\!\to\!1.40\!\to\!4.79\!\to\!11.56$ across $\sobs{=}0,0.1,0.2,0.4$ --- so at
high noise it runs the guarded fallback for a large share of its $50$ steps. We
\emph{hypothesize} (but have not shown by a guardian-removal ablation) that this
conservatism drives the robustness. Its weakness is $\sobs{=}0$ ($4.37$): the
held-out OOD quantile and the safety budget are over-conservative when the state is
essentially observed, so it forgoes return it could safely take.

\paragraph{(4) Delphic underperforms and does not show the predicted signature.}
Delphic is the weakest method under the learned filter ($5.27$ overall; the $4.54$
\texttt{model\_return\_mean} in \texttt{aggregate.json} is a filter-\emph{mixed}
aggregate and must \emph{not} be used for ranking, since PLUS/MOOR have three filter
variants and the other methods two) with a non-monotone, noisy profile (a dip to
$3.74$ at $\sobs{=}0.1$, high unsafe occupancy $0.112$ at $\sobs{=}0$), and its
beats-both rate never exceeds $0.17$. The compatible-worlds construction is
currently random-feature-linear with the latent scaled by posterior ambiguity;
while this gives the correct $\sobs{=}0$ negative control ($u_\Delta\!\to\!0$), its
random-world uncertainty $u_\Delta$ at $\sobs{>}0$ does not track confounding
strength ($\sobs$) monotonically (there is no ordinary bootstrap ensemble in the
current compatible-world axis), and the linear CQL head is conservative. Delphic's
hypothesized monotone advantage with $\sobs$ is \emph{not} observed.

\paragraph{(5) The shared filter helps the learners and is a plausible bottleneck.}
The ablation lifts the dynamics-learning methods ($+0.3$ to $+1.5$ vs.\ raw) and is
roughly neutral for the mechanistic baselines vs.\ raw; the Ricker-filter check
shows PLUS is filter-insensitive but MOOR gains $+0.40$ from the shared filter, so
``fair'' holds cleanly only for PLUS. Filter RMSE rises to $\approx 160$ at
$\sobs{=}0.4$, comparable to the state scale, which we \emph{hypothesize} caps the
learned methods --- this is not yet demonstrated; an oracle-filter ablation (feed
the true $s$) would test it directly and has not been run.

\paragraph{(6) BA-MCTS trades return for risk near the boundary.} BA-MCTS has the
highest collapse rate ($.114$--$.185$) and degrades most with noise, yet it wins
the most cells at $\sobs{=}0.1$ ($0.83$). We \emph{hypothesize} its zero-value
leaves and optimistic expansion under-penalize near-threshold actions once the
belief is noisy; a leaf-value ablation (fitted belief-value vs.\ zero) would be
needed to confirm.

\section{Limitations and proposed improvements}
\label{sec:improve}
Ordered by expected impact on the central question (restoring a learned advantage
under noise) and on faithfulness.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Stronger belief filter.} The PF transition proposal is a linear
log-space model; at $\sobs{=}0.4$ RMSE $\approx 160$ caps every learner. Add a
learned recurrent/state-space encoder (already scoped as the optional second
backend) or a heavier proposal; re-run the noise sweep. This is the most likely
lever to recover beats-both $>0$ at $\sobs{=}0.4$.
\item \textbf{Richer dynamics model.} The per-action quadratic ridge ensemble is
deliberately auditable but weak; a small MLP member (still composed with the exact
emission) should help MOPO/RefPlan/BA-MCTS/Delphic most where the belief is noisy.
\item \textbf{Delphic faithfulness + tuning.} Replace random-feature worlds with
worlds genuinely fit to tolerance under different confounding assumptions;
validate that $u_\Delta$ scales with $\sobs$ (the built-in confounding knob) and
not merely with bootstrap size; sweep $\beta_\Delta$ and CQL $\alpha$.
\item \textbf{OGSRL recalibration at low noise.} The deployment safety limit
reuses the discounted-budget value ($0.02$) as a one-step probability threshold (a
scale mismatch), and the guardian quantile is too tight at $\sobs{=}0$; calibrate
both per noise level to recover the $\sobs{=}0$ return without losing the
high-noise safety.
\item \textbf{BA-MCTS safety-aware leaves.} Replace zero terminal values with a
fitted belief-value (shared with RefPlan) and a near-threshold penalty to curb its
high collapse rate.
\item \textbf{Statistical power.} Beats-both is over $6$ cells/level (wide CIs).
Add independently trained datasets per cell (a robustness study) and report paired
bootstrap intervals as the headline, not point rates.
\item \textbf{Cross-noise comparability.} Operational and true returns agree only
\emph{after} method$\times\sobs$ averaging ($\le 0.10$); per episode they diverge
(up to $\sim 1$ at $\sobs{=}0.4$). Report $R^{\mathrm{true}}$ for any cross-$\sobs$
claim --- the median-one/mean-$>$one log-normal makes raw operational returns
non-comparable across $\sobs$ in principle (the $R^{\mathrm{true}}$ rankings here
match, so the central conclusion is unaffected).
\item \textbf{theta as graded control.} theta passes the diagnostic gate at all
$\sobs$; consider promoting it to a hard-gated headline cell now that it is
calibrated and decision-relevant.
\end{enumerate}
